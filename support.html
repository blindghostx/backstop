What Backstop does

Backstop is a pause button for the moment someone rushes you to pay. It checks a text, email or screenshot on your iPhone and explains the warning signs in plain words. It helps you ask the people you trust for a second look, gives you the words to say on a call that feels off, and reminds you to wait a day before paying anyone who asked you for money.

Below, Settings means Backstop's own settings: the gear at the top right of Backstop's home screen. The Settings app means your iPhone's Settings app.

Checking a message

How do I check a message?

Tap Something feel off? on Backstop's home screen. Paste the message, or tap Screenshot to pick a screenshot of it, then tap Check it. To try it first, tap Examples and pick one.

How does Backstop decide?

Backstop looks for 14 warning signs that come up again and again in scams:

  • gift cards as payment
  • crypto or a Bitcoin ATM
  • asking you for a code
  • wanting control of your computer
  • asking you to keep it secret
  • “new number” or “lost my phone”
  • claiming to be the government or police
  • a prize you have to pay for
  • money that's hard to get back, like wires, Zelle or cash apps
  • pressure to act right now
  • a family emergency story, like jail, bail or a hospital
  • threats to your account, like “suspended” or “unusual activity”
  • a link to an unfamiliar or look-alike site
  • a small fee to release something, like a toll or a delivery

Each sign adds to a score from 0 to 100, and three or more signs together add a little more. A score of 60 or more shows Likely scam, 30 to 59 shows Warning signs, and below 30 shows Looks normal. Backstop highlights the exact words it found and explains each sign in plain words.

Is my message sent anywhere?

No. The check runs on your iPhone, and it works without an internet connection. Screenshots are read on your iPhone too, with Apple's built-in text recognition, and the picture isn't saved or sent. Nobody sees the message unless you choose to ask your people.

It says “Looks normal”. Does that mean it's safe?

Not always. “Looks normal” means Backstop found none of the warning signs above, and scammers keep changing their words. If a message asks for money, a password or a code, ask your people anyway.

Backstop flagged a message I know is real.

That can happen, because real messages sometimes use the same words, like a bank alert about a locked card. Check with the sender using the app or phone number you already have for them, not the link or number in the message.

Checking from another app

How do I check a message without opening Backstop?

  1. Take a screenshot of the text or email. In Mail, Safari and most other apps you can skip this step.
  2. Tap the screenshot preview in the corner, or the app's Share button.
  3. Pick Backstop. The answer shows up right there, without leaving the app you're in.

Don't see Backstop? Swipe to the end of the row of apps, tap More, and turn Backstop on.

Checks you make this way are saved to your checks in Backstop. If you've added your people, you can ask them from there too. To start a 24-hour payment hold, open the Backstop app.

Ask your people

How do I add the people I trust?

During setup, tap Add from Contacts or Type a name and number. Later, in Settings › Your people, the buttons are Add from Contacts and Add by hand. Backstop uses Apple's contact picker, so it only sees the person you pick. To remove someone, swipe left on their name in Settings.

What does “Ask” send?

After a check, tap the Ask button (it shows your people's names). Backstop opens a text message to them that's already written. It says:

Can you look at this before I do anything? Backstop says: what Backstop found (scam risk score/100)

The message you checked, up to 400 characters

Please call me when you see this.

Nothing is sent until you tap Send. You can change the words first, or cancel. The text goes through Messages like any other, and your people don't need the app.

Backstop can also write a text to your people when you start or end a payment hold, and from the call coach. It works the same way: nothing goes until you tap Send.

Family phrase

What is the family phrase for?

A scammer can copy the sound of your grandchild's voice. A family phrase is something only your family knows. When a caller says they're family and in trouble, ask them for it. If they dodge it, get it wrong or say they forgot, hang up, and call them on the number saved in your phone.

How do I set or change it?

During setup, type a phrase or pick one of the ideas, and tap Save phrase. Later, in Settings › Family phrase, tap Set a phrase (or Change the phrase), type it or pick an idea, and tap Save. Pick something odd that isn't on social media. Tell it to your people out loud or on a call you started. Never text or email it.

Where is it stored?

In your iPhone's keychain, the same secure storage iOS uses for passwords. It isn't sent anywhere, and it isn't moved to a new iPhone, so set it again if you change phones.

How do I see it during a call?

Open I'm on a call, tap Family member, then tap Tap to see the answer.

On a call

How does the call coach work?

On Backstop's home screen, tap I'm on a call, or say to Siri, “I'm on a weird call in Backstop.” Pick who the caller says they are: Family member, My bank, Government or police or Tech support. Backstop shows you what to say now, and what to do next. For a family member, it tells you to ask for your family phrase. If you've added your people, a button writes them a text saying you're on a call that feels off.

How do I open Backstop while I'm on the phone?

Tap Speaker so you can still hear the caller. Then go to your Home Screen (swipe up from the bottom of the screen, or press the Home button) and open Backstop. The call stays connected.

24-hour payment hold

What is the payment hold?

A promise to yourself to wait one day before paying anyone who asked you for money. Scams depend on rushing you, and a day gives you time to talk to someone you trust.

How do I start one?

When a check shows Likely scam or Warning signs, tap Wait 24 hours before paying at the bottom of the result. If you've added your people, Backstop also writes them a text about the hold so they can hold you to it (you still tap Send). Backstop's home screen shows ON HOLD and when the hold ends.

How does the reminder work?

The first time, Backstop asks if it can send you notifications. When the 24 hours are up, your iPhone shows “Your payment hold is over”, a reminder to ask your people before you pay. The reminder is set on your iPhone. If it doesn't appear, check that notifications are on in the Settings app, under Notifications › Backstop.

The hold is a promise, not a lock. It doesn't stop payments or contact your bank.

Can I end it early?

Yes. On Backstop's home screen, tap End the hold early. Backstop asks you to confirm, and if you've added your people, it writes them a text saying you ended it.

Scam text filter

How do I turn on the filter?

  1. Open the Settings app.
  2. Scroll down and tap Apps, then Messages.
  3. Tap Unknown & Spam.
  4. Under SMS Filtering, pick Backstop.

The same steps are in Backstop under Settings › Filter scam texts.

What does the filter do?

When a text arrives from a number that isn't in your contacts, iOS lets Backstop check its words. Only clear scams, the ones a check would show as Likely scam, go to the Junk folder in Messages. Everything else arrives as usual. Backstop never sees texts from your contacts.

Does the filter use the internet?

No. The filter checks each text on your iPhone and never uses the network. It doesn't save the texts it checks.

A real text went to Junk.

Texts moved to Junk aren't deleted, so look in the Junk folder in Messages if you're expecting a text that hasn't arrived. To stop filtering, go back to Unknown & Spam in the Settings app and pick None under SMS Filtering.

Past checks

How do I delete past checks?

Backstop keeps your 50 most recent checks on your iPhone. To delete one, tap See all checks on Backstop's home screen and swipe left on it. To delete them all, tap Clear all there, or Clear check history in Settings. Deleting Backstop also removes them.

Siri and widgets

Say “Something feels off in Backstop” to open the checker, or “I'm on a weird call in Backstop” for the call coach. The Something feel off? widget for your Home Screen or Lock Screen, and the Backstop button for Control Center, open the checker with one tap.

iPad

Backstop is made for iPhone. On iPad it runs in iPhone compatibility mode, so its screens are laid out like the iPhone app. Checking messages, the call coach, the family phrase, the payment hold and the scam drill work the same. Two things depend on an iPhone:

  • Texting your people. If your iPad can send messages with iMessage, Ask opens a message as usual. If it can't, Ask opens the share sheet instead, so you can send the text another way, like Mail.
  • The scam text filter. It needs an iPhone and isn't available on iPad.

What Backstop can't do

  • It doesn't report crimes, and it doesn't contact the police, your bank or anyone else for you. Texts to your people only go when you tap Send.
  • It doesn't block calls or stop payments.
  • It can miss scams, and it can flag real messages. Scammers change their words all the time.

When in doubt, call a family member, or your bank on a number you know, like the one on the back of your card. Don't use a number or link from the message. If you've already paid or shared a code, call your bank on that number right away.

Privacy

Backstop doesn't collect your information. Your checks, your people and your family phrase stay on your iPhone. Read the privacy policy.
